## 7 Top Stocks to Buy and Hold for the Next Decade and Beyond

**Source:** Lyn Alden - Investment Strategy

**Key points:**

- Enterprise Products Partners (EPD): a diversified midstream pipeline giant with a 7.5%+ distribution yield, 25 years of distribution growth, low commodity exposure, and a wide moat from pipeline infrastructure.
- Brookfield Corporation (BN): a global alternative-asset manager with real assets (property, infrastructure, renewables) that buys distressed assets cheaply during downturns and compounds capital, though its complex structure looks overvalued at first glance.
- MicroStrategy (MSTR): a business analytics firm turned bitcoin treasury company; performance is tied directly to bitcoin's success, making it the most speculative and controversial pick.
- HDFC Bank (HDB), Canadian Natural Resources (CNQ), CVS Health (CVS), and Intercontinental Exchange (ICE) round out the list for exposure to Indian banking growth, long-life oil sands reserves, vertically-integrated healthcare, and exchange/clearing-house network effects, respectively, all chosen for strong moats, balance sheets, and reasonable valuations.

**Link:** https://www.lynalden.com/top-stocks-to-buy/

## The Best Companies to Own

**Source:** Morningstar

**Key points:**

- Morningstar's list highlights companies with wide economic moats, standard-or-exemplary capital allocation, and low-to-medium fair value uncertainty, believed capable of generating excess returns for 20+ years.
- Companies are grouped by sector—Consumer Cyclical, Consumer Defensive, Financial Services, Healthcare, Industrials, Technology, and other sectors (basic materials, communications, energy)—with Industrials contributing the most names.
- Featured tickers mentioned include Tyler Technologies, GE Aerospace, General Dynamics, C.H. Robinson Worldwide, and Rollins, among 150+ others covered by Morningstar analysts.
- Moats stem from network effects, intangible assets (patents/brands), cost advantages, switching costs, or efficient scale; the article stresses that even great companies can be bad investments if overpaid for, directing readers to a separate 'most undervalued' list.

**Link:** https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/best-companies-own-2026-edition

## Quality stocks for uncertain times

**Source:** Fidelity Investments

**Key points:**

- Fidelity fund managers favor high-quality companies with strong brands, deep competitive moats, pricing power, and predictable recurring revenues (e.g., subscriptions) to navigate 2026's policy and macro uncertainty.
- Named examples include Costco (membership-fee driven, low-margin moat), Verisk Analytics (subscription-based insurance analytics), and Amazon (Prime subscriptions plus AWS cloud growth), all cited for low earnings volatility.
- The traditional line between 'blue chip' and 'growth' stocks has blurred; managers cite NVIDIA, Alphabet, Meta, Netflix, Shopify, and Roblox as modern examples of large companies still compounding via network effects and 'flywheel' dynamics.
- Fund manager Dan Sherwood highlights temporarily undervalued quality in health care and applications software, naming Descartes Systems and Veeva Systems as vertical-software companies deeply embedded with customers despite AI disruption fears.

**Link:** https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/trading-investing/investing-in-quality
